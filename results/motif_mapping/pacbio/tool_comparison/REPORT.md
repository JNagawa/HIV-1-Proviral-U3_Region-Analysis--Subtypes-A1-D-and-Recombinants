# Motif-mapping tool comparison -- PacBio HIV-SMRTcap arm

Generated by `scripts/motif_mapping/pacbio/compare_motif_tools_pacbio.sh`.
Inputs: the U3 sequences extracted by `compare_motif_mapping_pacbio.sh` from the
MAFFT alignment of the four SMRTcap samples plus HXB2 (K03455.1).

## 1. Read this first: only two of the four samples contribute real sequence

The assembly stage builds its consensus with `bcftools consensus`, which applies
called variants **onto the HXB2 reference**. Positions with no read coverage are
emitted as reference bases, not as N -- which is why every consensus is exactly
9719 bp at 0.00% N regardless of how little of the genome the sample actually
covered. Mapping the stripped proviral cores back to HXB2 shows the samples cover
very different intervals, and two of them never reach the U3 window at all.

    sequence  length  identical_to_HXB2_U3  pct_identity  interpretation
    K03455.1  453     reference             100.00        the reference itself
    124_4     453     yes                   100.00        IDENTICAL to HXB2 -- no sample-specific sequence here
    128_5     453     yes                   100.00        IDENTICAL to HXB2 -- no sample-specific sequence here
    203_3     453     no                    84.99         sample-specific sequence present
    211_0     453     no                    86.53         sample-specific sequence present

**Consequence:** 124_4 and 128_5 carry a U3 that is byte-identical to HXB2's, so
three of the five scanned sequences are the same sequence. Any tool will agree
with itself on those, and per-sequence hit counts for K03455.1, 124_4 and 128_5
are identical for every tool -- confirming duplication rather than reproducibility.
**The effective sample size for this comparison is n=2 (203_3 and 211_0).**

### FIMO vs TFBSTools -- the requested pair

FIMO called 16 sites; TFBSTools called 87. **All 16 FIMO calls are reproduced by
TFBSTools at byte-identical coordinates** (16 exact, 0 partial, 0 FIMO-only), while
TFBSTools adds 62 further calls. FIMO is therefore a strict subset of TFBSTools:
where both fire they agree perfectly on position, and the entire difference between
them is stringency, not disagreement about where sites are. The base-pair Jaccard of
0.31 reflects that extra breadth, not positional conflict.

### MOODS

11 of MOODS's 12 calls exactly match a FIMO call; 5 FIMO calls have no MOODS
counterpart and 1 MOODS call is unique. The three PWM scanners thus place sites
consistently; they differ almost entirely in how many they report.

### gquad vs pqsfinder -- the requested G4 pair

gquad predicted 15 G-quadruplexes, pqsfinder 5. **Not one pair shares identical
boundaries** (0 exact), yet every pqsfinder call overlaps a gquad call (5 partial,
0 pqsfinder-only) and 10 gquad calls have no pqsfinder counterpart. So the two agree
on roughly WHERE quadruplexes are but never on their extent -- a systematic
difference in how each defines a G4 boundary, not a sensitivity difference.

## 3. Sensitivity and specificity -- what can and cannot be measured

Sensitivity and specificity require a gold standard: a curated list of sites that
are genuinely present and genuinely absent. **No such truth set exists for these
sample U3 sequences**, so those two statistics cannot honestly be computed for the
subset, and the concordance figures in section 2 must not be read as sensitivity.

The one place real ground truth exists is HXB2's own U3, whose core regulatory
elements are established in the literature: two NF-kB sites, three Sp1 sites and
the TATA box. Positions below are derived from their published offsets upstream of
the U3/R boundary, converted to coordinates in the 453bp extracted U3. A tool is
credited when a call overlaps the landmark window; the windows are approximate to
within a few bp, so this is indicative, not a validated benchmark.

    landmark  u3_start  u3_end  fimo  tfbstools  moods  gquad  pqsfinder  
    NFkB-II   350       359     yes   yes        yes    yes    -          
    NFkB-I    364       373     yes   yes        yes    yes    yes        
    Sp1-III   376       386     -     yes        -      yes    yes        
    Sp1-II    387       397     yes   yes        -      yes    yes        
    Sp1-I     398       408     -     yes        -      yes    yes        
    TATA      426       430     -     yes        -      -      -          

**Recovery of the 6 known landmarks:** TFBSTools 6/6, FIMO 3/6, MOODS 2/6.

On the only ground truth available, TFBSTools is the most sensitive TFBS scanner
and FIMO the most conservative -- FIMO misses the TATA box and two of the three
Sp1 sites that TFBSTools recovers. Because FIMO's calls are a strict subset of
TFBSTools's, TFBSTools cannot be less sensitive than FIMO on any input.

**Specificity cannot be separated from this.** The 62 calls TFBSTools makes beyond
FIMO's set are either genuine weaker sites or false positives, and nothing in this
data distinguishes the two. Higher sensitivity with unknown precision is the honest
summary, not "TFBSTools is better".

The G4 tools also overlap several TFBS landmarks. That is not a scoring error:
the Sp1/NF-kB region of the HIV-1 LTR is GC-rich and carries documented
G-quadruplex structures (LTR-III/LTR-IV), so genuine G4 predictions are expected
to coincide with it. Their landmark column is shown for completeness but should
not be read as TFBS detection.

## 4. Practical conclusions

- **FIMO vs TFBSTools:** identical coordinates wherever both call, so the choice is
  purely one of stringency. Use FIMO when false positives are costly; use TFBSTools
  when missing a weak site is costly. Reporting both is redundant -- FIMO adds no
  site TFBSTools lacks.
- **gquad vs pqsfinder:** these are not interchangeable. They locate the same
  regions but assign different extents, so any downstream analysis keyed on G4
  boundaries (overlap with a TFBS, distance to a TSS) will change with the tool.
  Fix one tool for the whole study rather than mixing.
- **Before drawing biological conclusions**, fix the coverage problem in section 1:
  the consensus step should emit N (or be masked) where read depth is zero, so a
  sample cannot silently contribute reference sequence to a motif scan.
